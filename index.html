About

I taught before I could code: first scuba diving in Belize, then high school English in Seattle. In both, you learn fast that the quietest student is often the one who got lost 4 steps back and is too embarrassed to say so.

Then I learned to code, badly, and became that student. I know exactly what it feels like to read documentation written by someone who has forgotten what it was like not to know.

That's the job, as far as I'm concerned. When an academy I built lost 492 learners, not one of them complained. They all quit at the same step, environment setup, before writing a line of code, which meant it was fixable, and we fixed it.

No one lists on a resume that they were once bad at the thing they now teach, but it's the most useful qualification I have.
